# Changelog

## 0.2.0

- **Why a session needs you.** A waiting session says what kind of wait it is and quotes the ask: a permission with the tool and its command or file (`Bash: npm test`), a question with its answers, a plan to approve, or another dialog with the agent's own word for it. The cards, the jump list, the status bar's hover and the notification all show it. A session of this window whose last reply asks you something is counted as needing you too, after the prompts, on a dashed card marked "asked you a question", until it works again or you dismiss it with the card's ✓ (`lookout.state.typedQuestions`, on by default); it is announced once, like a prompt, when you were not looking.
- **Where a state comes from.** A card's hover says whether its state comes from the session file, the transcript or the process, and how old that is. A session file that records no state, or cannot be read, leaves the session "State unknown". A working session whose transcript has been silent for more than 10 minutes reads "Working, quiet 12m": its card stops moving, and the status bar counts it with a clock instead of the spinner.
- **Desktop notifications.** While VS Code is not the window in front, what the notifications announce also shows on the desktop: notify-send on Linux, Notification Center on macOS, a Windows toast on Windows and from WSL (`lookout.notifications.os`, on by default), with a short sound if you want one (`lookout.notifications.sound`). It cannot jump, so it names the key that does. At most one per session and event every 30 seconds (`lookout.notifications.cooldownSeconds`); while three or more sessions need you, one sums them up. SSH and container remotes have no desktop to reach and skip it.
- **Usage as the panel shows it.** The board and the status bar's hover list every limit as Claude Code's own panel names it, **Session (5hr)**, **Weekly (7 day)** and the per-model weekly ones such as **Weekly Fable**, each with its share used and "Resets in 3h"; the board lays each one out as the panel does, the label and share over the bar. The status bar's usage bar ends with the time to the 5-hour reset, `32% · 1h12m` (`lookout.statusBar.usageReset`, on by default). Lookout says once per window when the 5-hour window reaches 70% and 90% and the week 90%, in the VS Code window in front and on the desktop when VS Code is not (`lookout.usage.alerts`, on by default). Numbers more than 30 minutes old, or from before a reset, turn grey. A refresh runs at most once every 3 minutes on the machine, waits twice as long after each one that brings no new numbers, up to 30 minutes, and does not run while `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC` is set for Claude Code. It runs Claude Code with the config directory Lookout reads, and the numbers come from the cache it rewrites. Numbers cached for another account, or a cache that does not parse, show "No usage reported".
- **Context pressure.** A card's context ring turns amber from 75% and red from 90%, and a pin on its pulse marks the minute the agent compacted the conversation.
- **Jump to another window's session.** A card, or an entry in the jump list, of a session hosted by another VS Code window on the same machine jumps to it: that window brings the session's tab or terminal forward and comes to the front; when the desktop does not let it, Lookout says which window to switch to. Sessions in other windows' terminals are now named after their window too, not "outside VS Code": every window publishes its terminals' shells next to its name. Windows on the same machine and in the same VS Code profile see each other.
- **Every project's history.** A toggle on the Earlier heading lists the sessions of this window's folders or of every project on the machine (kept per workspace), and the search follows it; the newest 500 are listed, with a note when there are more. Cards, earlier ones included, show the git branch, and the conversation tab's heading shows the project and branch. The search takes operators: `since:` and `before:` (`today`, `yesterday`, `12h`, `3d`, `2w`, `2026-10-01`), `project:`, `branch:`, and `file:` for the paths the agent's tool calls worked on; operators alone list the sessions they keep. A session of another project opens its conversation rather than an empty tab, since the agent resumes it only in a window open on its folder.
- **A search page.** Every conversation's matches in an editor tab, with the room to read them: `Lookout: Search All Sessions`, the search icon on the board's title bar, or a click on the board's match count. Sessions with matches are listed newest match first, with their project, branch and whether they are running, and up to 20 matches each, cut wider than on the cards; a click opens the conversation at that message. The page takes the board's operators.
- **Favourites.** A small star on every card stars the session; starred sessions are gathered at the top of the board, running or not and whatever project they belong to, and a click jumps to them, resumes them, or opens their conversation. Stars are kept for the machine, so every window shows them.
- **Tabs restored after a reload.** The agent restores its tabs after a window reload but starts a session only once its tab is opened; until then the board keeps the session, idle, on a card that says "Tab open · not started", and a click opens the tab. A tab or terminal with nothing sent is left off the board while it is idle.
- **A jump lands on the newest message.** A jump to a session in the agent's panel, from a card, the jump list or a notification, brings its tab to the front at the newest message, with the cursor in the prompt box, or at the prompt it waits on, through Claude Code's own "Focus last message" and "Focus input"; it no longer opens where the tab was last scrolled.
- **Finished turns of terminal sessions.** A session in a terminal that finished a turn while you were in a tab or an editor was never marked finished, and a mark it did get cleared at the next change of tabs or window focus: VS Code keeps a terminal its active one while you work elsewhere. Lookout now goes by where you were last: the terminal you brought forward or prompted a session in, or a tab, an editor or a panel session.

## 0.1.0

First release.

- **Board.** A view in the activity bar. At the top, the harbour: a night scene with the lighthouse, its sweeping beam, and the sessions as boats on the water, placed by state and by time in that state; the beam lights the boats it passes and swings to a boat before a jump, and the tide rises with the 5-hour usage. Below, one card per session, grouped by state, showing what each session is doing, its tool calls per minute over the last 15 minutes (fewer while it is younger than that, so the bars grow from the left), how full its context window is, the tokens it has used with their price at API list rates, its project, where it runs and its model. Click a boat or a card to jump; rename a session from its card through the agent's own rename; hide a state group with a click on the legend. A tab with nothing sent yet is listed as "Empty tab"; a session in its first minute counts as idle.
- **Earlier sessions.** Under the live sessions, a collapsed group holds the sessions of the window's folders that are not running and that Claude Code has not archived; a click on the heading opens it, a click on a session resumes it in the agent's panel.
- **Search.** A search box above the cards looks through every conversation, live and earlier, what you wrote and what the agent replied. Sessions with matches stay on the board, each with its newest three matches under the card and the words marked; the rest hide until the box is cleared. Every word must appear in one message. A click on a snippet, or on "+N more", opens the session's whole conversation in a tab, scrolled to the match; the tab wears the harbour's night colours and renders the messages' Markdown; it has its own search with next and previous, dims what does not match, follows the conversation as it grows, and has a button to bring the session's own tab or terminal forward.
- **Status bar.** Counts of sessions that are working, waiting for you, and finished since you last looked, with a spinner while any session works and a highlighted background while any session waits. The 5-hour usage as a ten-cell bar coloured by consumption, green, amber from half, red from 80%; the week and the per-model limits stay in the hover. The hover list shows every session's title, project, worktree and state.
- **Jump.** `Lookout: Jump to Session` (`Ctrl+Alt+J`) lists the sessions, the ones that need attention first, with how long each has been in its state, and brings the chosen one's tab or terminal to the front. `Lookout: Jump to Next Session That Needs You` (`Ctrl+Alt+Shift+J`) goes straight to the one that has waited longest and cycles through the rest on repeated presses.
- **Finished turns.** A session that finishes a turn while you are not looking at it is marked finished until you open its tab or terminal; the marks survive a window reload. `Lookout: Mark All Sessions as Seen` clears them.
- **Notifications.** A notification with a Jump button when a session starts to wait for you (`lookout.notifications.waiting`, on by default) or finishes a turn (`lookout.notifications.done`, off by default).
- **Usage.** The 5-hour and weekly limits in the status bar item and on the board (bars coloured by consumption: amber from half, red from 80%), from Claude Code's cache. Claude Code fetches them again when they are older than 10 minutes (`lookout.usage.refresh`: `auto`, `manual` or `off`); `Lookout: Refresh Usage` fetches at once.
- **Agents.** Claude Code: sessions in the VS Code panel and in integrated terminals. Sessions in another VS Code window are listed with that window's name.
- **Icon.** The lighthouse is the mark everywhere: the activity bar, and the status bar item through an icon font the extension ships.
- **Platforms.** Linux, WSL, Linux remotes, and Windows.
